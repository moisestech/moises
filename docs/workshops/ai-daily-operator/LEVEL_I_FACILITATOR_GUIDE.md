# Level I Facilitator Guide — Build Your AI Daily Operator

**Method:** Founder Attention OS  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Platforms:** ChatGPT or Claude  
**Integrations:** None required  
**Primary outcome:** Every participant leaves with a Founder Profile, Attention Rules, a portable Daily Operator, a first Daily Operating Brief, and one observed friction.

## Facilitator principle

Do not teach “how to use all of AI.”

Teach one operating loop:

`Goal → Context → Judgment → Attention → Action → Review`

The workshop succeeds when a participant can explain **why** one thing deserves attention more than another.

## Before the room opens

Ask participants to bring:

- a laptop or phone with ChatGPT or Claude;
- one real 30-day business outcome;
- tomorrow/today’s calendar or schedule;
- a short list of deadlines, follow-ups, open opportunities, and tasks;
- no passwords, payment details, confidential records, or sensitive client information.

Keep the synthetic case ready for anyone who should not use real business information.

Open:

1. Program overview
2. Level I participant toolkit
3. ChatGPT or Claude
4. Synthetic case if needed

## Privacy / data note to read aloud

Use your own business information only when it is appropriate and safe. Do not paste passwords, payment credentials, confidential customer records, private personnel information, or information your organization does not permit you to share with an AI tool. You may use the synthetic practice case instead. A polished answer is still a draft to review.

## Minute-by-minute

### 0–10 — One outcome

Ask:

> What meaningful business result should be different 30 days from now?

Do not accept a task list as the goal.

Examples:

- “Rewrite the website” is a task.
- “Generate five qualified inbound leads from the website” is an outcome.

### 10–25 — Founder Profile

Participants run the progressive interview.

Facilitator watches for:

- endless intake-question dumps;
- overly personal information;
- vague business model;
- no distinction between founder-only work and delegable work;
- missing approval boundaries.

Intervene with:

> What is the smallest missing piece of context that would change the recommendation?

### 25–35 — Attention Rules

Teach four lenses:

1. Commitments
2. Revenue / Mission
3. Risk
4. Capacity

Rule:

> If the rules make everything important, they are not rules yet.

### 35–45 — Daily Operator

Participants convert their profile and rules into portable instructions.

Emphasize:

- the prompt is not the product;
- the business context and judgment method are the durable asset;
- missing is unknown, not false;
- recommend → explain → ask → act.

### 45–58 — Run a real day

Participants manually provide their current day.

Manual entry is intentional.

Do not connect Calendar, QuickBooks, or CRM yet.

The purpose is to prove whether the decision model is useful before adding infrastructure.

### 58–67 — Challenge the ranking

Everyone asks:

> Why #1 instead of #2?

Require the response to make the tradeoff through the four lenses.

If the model cannot justify the ranking, the participant has learned something valuable about missing context or weak rules.

### 67–74 — Fact / Interpretation / Recommendation

Take the highest-priority item.

Require three buckets:

- FACT
- INTERPRETATION
- RECOMMENDATION

A fluent inference must not move into FACT.

### 74–82 — Find one friction

Ask:

> What did I have to retrieve, remember, reconcile, transfer, decide, approve, or rewrite manually during this exercise?

Do **not** recommend an automation yet.

### 82–88 — Choose one improvement

Permitted answers include:

- clarify a rule;
- improve the Founder Profile;
- establish a better source of truth;
- delegate;
- remove an unnecessary step;
- connect a source later;
- automate later.

### 88–90 — Seven-day experiment

Participants save their four artifacts and run the brief for seven days.

Daily correction questions:

1. What did the brief get wrong or miss?
2. What information did I have to reconstruct manually?

## Failure modes and fallbacks

### Participant has no clear goal

Ask:

> What decision or result is creating the most consequence in the next 30 days?

### Participant has no business data with them

Use the synthetic practice case.

### Participant cannot use ChatGPT

Use Claude.

### Participant cannot use Claude

Use ChatGPT.

### Tool is unavailable or account blocks a feature

Stay in basic chat. Level I requires no advanced feature.

### Participant wants to connect everything immediately

Say:

> First prove that the brief makes a better decision with information supplied manually. Connection comes after we know what information is actually useful.

### Participant asks AI to send/change/do something

Return to the Level I boundary:

> We are building judgment today, not autonomous action.

## Completion check

A participant passes Level I when they can demonstrate all of the following:

- name one current outcome;
- show a reviewed Founder Profile;
- show Attention Rules that force a tradeoff;
- run a Daily Operating Brief;
- explain why priority #1 outranks #2;
- distinguish fact, interpretation, and recommendation;
- name one observed friction;
- state one improvement for tomorrow;
- keep consequential action under human approval.

## Facilitator feedback to collect

After the session, record:

- Where did participants stall?
- Which field confused them?
- What did they skip?
- What did they over-share?
- Did “Daily Operator” make sense without explanation?
- Did the Daily Brief change a real decision?
- Which friction type appeared most often?
- Did anyone ask for deeper integrations?
- Would the host book Levels II–V?
- What single course change would create the largest improvement?
