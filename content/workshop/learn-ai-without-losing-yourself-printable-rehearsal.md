## Core tone reminder

You are **inside the contradiction** with the audience.

You are not:

- selling AI salvation
- condemning AI from a distance
- doing stand-up about tools
- giving a dry software tutorial

You are:

- funny through recognition
- honest about dependency
- critical of optimization pressure
- useful and practical
- self-aware about labor, time, and the weirdness of becoming more machine-legible

### Emotional mix

- humor
- honesty
- trust
- narrative tension
- practical skill
- quiet discomfort

### Repeating teaching rhythm

For every section:

**Human problem → AI temptation → funny misuse → self-aware critique → practical takeaway**

# OPENING

## 0:00–5:00

### Goal

Frame the room as tired, pressured, and already inside technological acceleration. Introduce confidence, algorithmic intimacy, machine-speed communication, and the talk’s method.

---

## Opening script

“Quick question before we begin:

How many of you started using AI because you were curious?

And how many of you started using it because you were already behind?”

**[pause]**

“Yeah.

That’s the room.

Not the room of futurists.
Not the room of people saying, ‘Finally, the future has arrived.’

The room of people with:

- too many emails
- too many tabs
- too many things to summarize
- too many things to make sound better
- and not enough time to be a full person while doing any of it.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“I think that matters.

Because most people did not arrive here through philosophy.

They arrived here through some version of:

## ‘I need help right now.’”

**[pause]**

“And that’s why AI is weird.

Because it really is helpful.

But it’s also one of the only things I can think of that can help you while quietly making you feel judged by how much help you apparently need.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“Like:
great, thank you for helping me write this email.

I did not realize I also needed help becoming the kind of person who sends emails like that.”

**[pause]**

“These tools are not entering our lives in some neutral way.

They’re arriving at the exact point where a lot of us are already thinking:

- I don’t want to write this
- I don’t want to summarize this
- I don’t want to pretend I deeply understand this meeting
- I don’t want to brainstorm on command
- I don’t want to sound confused
- I don’t want to sound rude
- I don’t want to sound replaceable
- I just need to get through the day without embarrassing myself professionally”

**[pause for recognition]**

“And then the machine shows up like:

## ‘I can help with that.’”

**[pause]**

“Which is incredible.

And also slightly insulting.”

---

## Transition into confidence

“One of the things I’ve been noticing is that AI is not just changing how fast we work.

It is changing what confidence feels like.”

**[pause]**

“Because confidence used to mean something like:

- I studied
- I practiced
- I did the work
- I know this well enough to stand behind it

It was a relationship.

Your boss feels confident you can do the task.
You feel confident going into the presentation.
You feel confident because you actually understand what you are saying.”

**[pause]**

“And now a machine can give you:

- the tone of confidence
- the shape of confidence
- the formatting of confidence
- the vibe of readiness

before you actually know.”

**[pause]**

“So one of the weirdest things AI can do is:

## generate the feeling of confidence faster than it can generate understanding.”

**[pause]**

“And that’s not abstract.

That’s every clean summary.
Every polished outline.
Every convincing paragraph you suddenly feel ready to repeat.”

---

## Transition into algorithmic intimacy

“And meanwhile the systems around us are getting weirder in a very intimate way.

AI can’t do my laundry yet.

But it can sort my bills, my emails, my notes, my reminders, my calendar — and at this point I’m not fully convinced the algorithm doesn’t know more about some of my patterns than I do.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“The machine cannot fold my clothes.

But it can increasingly clean up after my cognition.”

**[pause]**

“So this is no longer just about using a tool.

It’s about living in an environment where systems are:

- organizing us
- predicting us
- shaping our pace
- nudging our choices
- formatting our communication
- and increasingly deciding what counts as ‘ready’”

---

## Transition into machine-speed communication

“And there’s another layer.

There used to be:
human talking to human.

Now there is:
human talking to AI.

And more and more:
AI talking to AI.”

**[pause]**

“And one of the strangest parts of this moment is that information now moves faster between machines than understanding moves between people.

The files are syncing.

The humans are still buffering.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“That’s not just a technical shift.

That’s a cultural shift.

Because communication is no longer just:
meaning,
conversation,
expression.

It is also:
instruction,
format,
protocol,
something a machine can parse, summarize, rank, store, and send back to you looking calmer than you feel.”

---

## Transition into acceleration / environment

“And this is part of why the whole thing feels hard to avoid now.

It’s not just in our browser tabs.

You start seeing robot cars.
Self-driving taxis.
Delivery bots.

And at a certain point you realize technological acceleration is no longer a debate.

It’s becoming traffic.”

**[pause]**

“You can tell yourself you’re taking your time with all this…

until the car with no driver pulls up calmer than you have ever been in your life.”

**[pause for laugh]**

---

## Set up the talk structure

“So this is not a talk about AI being evil.

And it’s not a talk about AI being amazing.

It’s a talk about the uncomfortable middle.

The place where:

- the help is real
- the convenience is real
- the speed is real
- the leverage is real

and also:

- the flattening is real
- the dependence is real
- the false confidence is real
- the maintenance labor is real
- and the pressure underneath all of it is very real”

**[pause]**

“When I say **Learn AI Without Losing Yourself**, I mean:

how do you use these tools
without becoming:

- flatter
- smoother
- more optimized
- more generic
- more machine-legible
- and somehow less recognizable to yourself?”

**[pause]**

“Because I don’t think losing yourself happens in one dramatic science-fiction moment.

I think it happens quietly.

You stop writing the sentence yourself.
You stop checking whether you actually understand what you just summarized.
You stop noticing when every idea starts sounding equally polished and equally dead.
You stop hearing your own voice because everything now sounds ‘good enough.’

And under pressure…

good enough starts sounding like a miracle.”

**[pause]**

“So tonight I want to look at a few very normal situations where people reach for AI:

- writing
- research
- brainstorming
- and then the really weird one:
  what happens when AI starts to feel less like a tool and more like a staff”

**[pause]**

“And in each case, I want to look at four things:

## pressure

## prompt

## problem

## practice

What is the human problem?
What is the AI temptation?
What weird thing happens?
And what does a better relationship to the tool actually look like?”

**[pause]**

“The point here is not purity.

Most of us are going to use these tools.

I use them.

The point is not to pretend the tool doesn’t work.

The point is to stay awake inside the usefulness.”

**[pause]**

## “Use AI for assistance, not surrender.”

**[pause]**

“And let’s start with one of the most dangerous social situations in modern life:

## writing something professional.”

---

# SEGMENT 1

## Writing Without Sounding Possessed

### 5:00–10:00

### Goal

Teach **prompting basics**, **tone control**, and **voice preservation**.

### Skill words to emphasize

**Prompting**
**Tone**
**Structure**
**Voice**

---

## Segment 1 script

“Let’s start with professional writing, because I think this is one of the first places people quietly hand part of themselves over.

Not writing a book.

I mean:

- an email
- a Slack message
- a follow-up
- a response that needs to sound warm, clear, competent, strategic, and emotionally stable

while you yourself are maybe not fully those things.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“This is where AI feels less like innovation and more like emergency emotional formatting.”

**[pause]**

“You type in your rough thoughts.
The machine gives you back something like:

‘I hope this finds you well…’
‘I wanted to thoughtfully follow up…’
‘I remain enthusiastic about aligning on next steps…’”

**[look up]**

“And immediately you’re like:

## this sounds like LinkedIn wrote it during a hostage situation.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“Or worse:
it sounds good enough.”

**[pause]**

“That’s the danger.

Not that it’s terrible.
That it’s acceptable.

That it sounds:

- polished
- calm
- competent
- employable

but also like absolutely nobody.”

**[pause]**

“It removes all evidence that a person with stakes was ever involved.”

---

## Transition into teaching the skill

“So here’s the better method.

Not:

## ‘Write this for me.’

More like:

## ‘Help me structure what I actually mean.’”

**[pause]**

“My basic move is this:

### First, write the human core yourself.

Usually just three bullets:

- what happened
- what I need
- what tone this should have

For example:

- appreciated the meeting
- need clarity on next step
- would like an answer by Friday

That part is human.
That part is the point.”

**[pause]**

“Then I use AI for:

- **structure**
- **shortening**
- **tone adjustment**
- **two or three variations**”

**[pause]**

“And then I do one more thing:

## put one human sentence back.”

“One sentence with actual life in it.
One sentence that sounds like I was there.
One sentence the machine would not invent unless it had my memory, my mood, and my exact stakes.”

**[pause]**

“Because the danger is not just bad writing.

## The danger is when efficiency starts replacing sincerity.”

**[pause]**

“AI can help format your thought.

Do not let it remove all evidence that you were here.”

---

## Transition to Segment 2

“So okay — now maybe the email sounds human enough.

Unfortunately, that does not mean we understand anything.

Which brings us to research.”

---

## Optional alt jokes for Segment 1

- “This is not warmth. This is strategic moisturization.”
- “I asked for help writing a message and got managerial perfume.”
- “AI is incredible at making you sound like someone who has slept.”
- “It’s not wrong. It’s just written by someone I would never trust.”
- “This sounds emotionally healthy in a way that makes me suspicious.”

# SEGMENT 2

## Research Without Fake Mastery

### 10:00–18:00

### Goal

Teach **summarization**, **verification**, **confidence checks**, **anti-sycophancy**, and **stopping rules**.

### Skill words to emphasize

**Summarization**
**Verification**
**Confidence check**
**Friction**
**Stopping rules**

---

## Segment 2 script

“Research is really just a nicer word for:

## ‘I need to understand this fast enough to survive the conversation.’”

**[pause for laugh]**

“You have:

- an article
- a report
- a deck
- a transcript
- a meeting
- a topic you were supposed to know about yesterday

So you ask AI to summarize it.

And it does.

Beautifully.

Almost too beautifully.”

**[pause]**

“This is one of my favorite AI genres:

## the emotional experience of knowledge.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“Because now I have:

- three bullet points
- five takeaways
- and a tone of confidence I did not earn”

**[pause]**

“A summary is not understanding.

Sometimes it is just confusion with better formatting.”

---

## Transition into vibe-reading and confidence

“One of the strangest things AI can do is give you the feeling of confidence before you’ve actually earned understanding.

And confidence is not nothing.

Confidence is relational.

Your boss wants to feel confident you can do the task.
You want to feel confident going into the presentation.

But now the machine can generate:

- the tone of confidence
- the structure of confidence
- the posture of confidence

before you actually know.”

**[pause]**

“So the question becomes:

## do I understand this?

or

## do I just have a version of it that sounds ready?”

**[pause]**

“And I think this is where **vibe-reading** starts happening.

Sometimes we are no longer reading for truth.

We are reading for:

- coherence
- polish
- smartness
- presentability
- whether this sounds like it could survive a meeting”

**[pause]**

“That is vibe-reading.

And it’s dangerous.

Because vibe is not understanding.
Polish is not proof.
And readiness is not the same as knowledge.”

---

## Transition into practical method

“So here’s the first practical move:

### the **confidence check**.”

“After any summary, ask:

- What do I actually know here?
- What part of this came from the model?
- What might be missing or oversimplified?
- What should I verify independently?
- What could I explain in my own words without looking?”

**[pause]**

“And then ask the machine directly:

- What are you uncertain about?
- Which parts are inferred rather than grounded?
- What would a skeptical reader question here?
- What should not be trusted without checking?”

**[pause]**

“If it can’t survive those questions, it was never knowledge.

It was formatting.”

---

## Transition into hallucinations

“And sometimes the problem is not just flattening.

Sometimes the answer is wrong.

Not cartoonishly wrong.
Not obviously broken.

Just wrong in complete sentences.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“That’s what makes hallucinations so dangerous.

A hallucination is not always nonsense.

Sometimes it is:

- plausible
- smooth
- organized
- cite-shaped
- emotionally calm

It doesn’t just make things up.

It makes things up in a tone that suggests you should not interrupt.”

---

## Transition into sycophancy

“And then there’s **sycophancy**.

Which is a fancy way of saying:
the machine agrees with you too easily.”

**[pause]**

“You ask:

- is this a good idea?
- does this make sense?
- what do you think?

And depending on how you prompt, the assistant can become:

- too supportive
- too flattering
- too aligned
- too willing to continue the exact thing you were already doing”

**[pause]**

“Sometimes AI is not helping you think.

It is helping you continue.”

**[pause]**

“So here’s the second practical move:

### the **anti-sycophancy prompt**.”

“Ask:

- What is the strongest critique of this?
- What am I not seeing?
- Argue against my current assumption.
- What would a skeptical expert say?
- What are the failure modes?”

**[pause]**

“AI should not only support your momentum.

It should also interrupt your delusion.”

---

## Transition into continuity / stopping rules

“And then there’s **continuity**.

One of the most powerful things these tools can do is keep going.

They can continue the thread.
Keep the context alive.
Go deeper.

And that is both the superpower and the trap.”

**[pause]**

“At some point you are not solving the problem anymore.

You are cultivating a relationship with the problem.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“The machine can go deeper forever.

That does not mean you should.”

**[pause]**

“So the third practical move is:

### **stopping rules**.”

“Before you start, decide:

- what counts as success?
- what counts as done?
- how many rounds are enough?
- when do I stop prompting and move into a document?
- when do I ask another human?”

**[pause]**

“If you do not define an exit, continuity can become compulsion.”

---

## Segment 2 close

“So the principles here are:

## A summary is not understanding.

## Confidence is not comprehension.

## Sometimes we are not checking for truth — we are checking for the feeling of readiness.

## And continuity is the superpower and the trap.”

---

## Transition to Segment 3

“So now that we’ve looked at how AI can make us feel smarter than we are…

let’s move to creativity, where it can also make us feel more imaginative than we are.”

---

- “I’m now prepared to speak confidently about something I met six minutes ago.”
- “This didn’t make me smarter. It made me presentation-ready.”
- “It’s not just wrong. It’s wrong with excellent formatting.”
- “Sometimes the assistant is not your collaborator. It is your smartest enabler.”
- “At some point the thread is no longer helping you. It is using your afternoon.”

“Brainstorming is where AI becomes both impressive and spiritually suspicious.”

**[pause]**

“Because yes — it can give you twenty ideas in ten seconds.

And somehow that can still leave you feeling lonelier than before.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“You ask for:

- titles
- concepts
- workshop names
- campaign ideas
- prompts
- themes

And the machine says:

‘Absolutely. Here are 20 innovative ideas.’”

**[pause]**

“And 17 of them sound like they were generated for a conference called Future Now.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“This is not imagination.

This is autocomplete wearing a blazer.”

**[pause]**

“One thing AI is very good at is producing things that are usable enough to interrupt your patience, but not alive enough to replace your taste.”

**[pause]**

- **3 obvious ideas**
- **3 strange ideas**
- **3 wrong-but-interesting ideas**”

**[pause]**

**[pause]**

“Then sort them into:

- cliché
- usable
- alive

Because more options do not mean more imagination.

And abundance is not discernment.”

**[pause]**

“Slop rot is what happens when repeated exposure to polished sameness lowers your standards slowly enough that you don’t notice.”

**[pause]**

“The danger is not that AI gives you no ideas.

The danger is that it gives you enough ideas to stop waiting for a better one.”

## Segment 4 script

“Now we get to the really weird one.

The point where AI becomes:

- a writing assistant
- a research assistant
- a planning assistant
- a note-taking assistant
- a brainstorming assistant
- a ‘please make me sound employable by 4 PM’ assistant”

**[pause for laugh]**

“And the fantasy is obvious:

## I can scale myself.”

“I can do more.

I can be my own team.”

**[pause]**

## having ten assistants is not the same as having support.”

## Transition into boss-above / bot-below

“And this is where I noticed something that made me deeply uncomfortable.

With certain bosses — especially when they are vague and not technical — I sometimes feel like the AI.

They say:

- can you handle this?
- can you make this happen?
- can you turn this into something usable?

And my job is to take vague intention, translate it into actual execution, fill in the missing context, and return something clean.

Then I open ChatGPT…

and I become that exact same person.”

**[pause]**

## “With my boss, I feel like the AI.

With ChatGPT, I feel like the human.”

**[let that land]**

“That’s the loop.

At work I’m the machine.
At home I’m the manager.
Somewhere in the middle, I’m still supposed to be a person.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“My boss delegates to save time.
I delegate to AI to save time.

Everybody is just trying to recover an afternoon.”

**[pause]**

“So yes — use AI like a set of narrow helpers:

- drafting
- summarizing
- organizing
- comparing
- brainstorming

That part is real.

But do not confuse synthetic delegation with actual support.

And do not confuse scale with stability.”

**[pause]**

“The question is not whether the assistants help you.

The question is whether they become the justification for why you no longer get actual assistance.”

**[pause]**

## “Scaling yourself is not the same as saving yourself.”

# ADVANCED SYSTEMS MINI-SECTION

## When Help Becomes Infrastructure

### 27:00–34:45

### Goal

Teach **system prompts**, **projects / memory**, **scheduled prompts**, **cloning**, and **automation basics**.

### Skill words to emphasize

**System prompt**
**Context**
**Scheduled prompt**
**Pattern cloning**
**Automation**

---

## Mini-section script

“So far we’ve mostly talked about prompts like one-off requests.

Write this.
Summarize this.
Brainstorm this.

But once the tools start helping enough, something else happens.

You stop asking:

## ‘How do I use AI for this one task?’

And you start asking:

## ‘How do I set this up so it keeps helping me later?’”

**[pause]**

“And that is where AI stops feeling like a chatbot…

and starts feeling like infrastructure.”

---

### Part 1 — system prompts

“A normal prompt is:
help me do this.

A **system prompt** is more like:
here’s who you are, how you should behave, what tone to use, what to avoid, and what kind of output I trust from you.”

**[pause]**

“So basically:

## a system prompt is the assistant’s personality lease agreement.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“It’s how you tell the machine:

- you are a sharp editor
- you value brevity
- you avoid generic filler
- you don’t use startup clichés
- you structure things for this audience, not that one

That’s powerful.

Because now you’re not just asking for output.

You’re shaping behavior.”

**[pause]**

“Language is no longer just expression here.

It is also behavior architecture.”

---

### Part 2 — projects, memory, context

“Then you realize one chat is not enough.

Some work is not one task.

Some work has:

- a tone
- a history
- stable preferences
- recurring documents
- unfinished parts
- future versions”

**[pause]**

“And that’s where you start needing:

- chats
- projects
- memory
- files
- context”

**[pause]**

“One chat is a task.

A project is an ongoing container.

Memory is for stable truths.

Files are anchors.

And context is the difference between a useful assistant and a very polished stranger.”

**[pause]**

“At some point you are no longer using AI.

## You are managing an estate.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“My interior life now has folders.”

---

### Part 3 — scheduled prompts

“Then we get to one of my favorite bridges between conversation and workflow:

## scheduled prompts.”

**[pause]**

“This is where AI stops only answering questions…
and starts showing up on time.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“You can do things like:

- weekly review reminders
- monthly planning
- recurring grant checks
- ‘what should I stop doing?’ prompts
- deadline prep
- regular synthesis

And that’s fascinating because now the machine is not just participating in your language.

It’s participating in your time.”

**[pause]**

“That can be genuinely helpful.

Sometimes what people need is not more intelligence.

It’s better timing.”

**[pause]**

“But it also means self-management starts getting very weird.

Because now the tool is not just helping you think.

It is helping organize when you think.”

---

### Part 4 — cloning / digital twin

“And then there’s the most seductive one of all:

## cloning yourself.”

**[pause]**

“Or at least cloning parts of yourself:

- your tone
- your FAQs
- your style
- your recurring responses
- your project logic”

**[pause]**

“And this can be useful.

You can absolutely train an assistant to sound more like you.

Feed it examples.
Teach it your preferences.
Build a system prompt around your standards.

But this is where I think we need to be careful.

Because there is a big difference between:

## cloning the pattern

and

## replacing the person.”

**[pause]**

“A digital twin is useful right up until it starts sounding more patient than you.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“So the ethical move here is:

## clone the pattern, not the person.”

“Clone:

- formatting style
- tone range
- FAQ logic
- structure
- references

Do not pretend you cloned:

- judgment
- ethics
- accountability
- being there”

---

### Part 5 — Airtable / n8n / artifact generation

“And then, if you go one layer deeper, AI starts joining other systems.

This is where tools like:

- Airtable
- n8n
- docs
- forms
- PDFs
- automations

start to matter.”

**[pause]**

“Airtable gives you **structured memory**.

n8n gives you **delegated repetition**.

And AI in the middle can turn notes into:

- summaries
- one-pagers
- decks
- PDFs
- follow-ups
- records”

**[pause]**

“The machine is very good at turning panic into artifacts.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“Airtable is what happens when your to-do list gets institutional ambitions.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“And automation is what happens when you get tired of manually disappointing yourself.”

**[pause for laugh]**

“But here’s the catch:

## automation solves repetition

## and creates maintenance”

“You wanted freedom.

Now you have:

- workflows
- triggers
- exceptions
- prompt upkeep
- syncing problems

# Helpful improvements for the edutainment version

A few things that will make this stronger on stage:

## 1. Emphasize the skill word each time

Say it out loud:

- “The skill here is **prompting**.”
- “The skill here is **verification**.”
- “The skill here is **selection**.”
- “The skill here is **task boundaries**.”
- “The skill here is **context design**.”

That helps the audience feel they are learning, not just listening.

## 2. Use one bridge line between every segment

Examples:

- “Now that we can sound professional, let’s ask whether we know anything.”
- “Now that we feel informed, let’s ask whether we still have taste.”
- “Now that we can generate ideas, let’s ask what happens when the tool starts feeling like a team.”
- “Now that the team exists, let’s ask what happens when help becomes infrastructure.”
- “After automation and interpassivity — when the system can even perform the ‘handled’ feeling for you — what should still belong to a person?”

## 3. Add one sentence of explanation after each big quote slide

This is what you asked for, and it helps a lot.
For example:

- after “A summary is not understanding,” add:
  “A summary is useful, but usefulness is not the same thing as ownership.”
- after “10 assistants ≠ support,” add:
  “Delegation can scale output and still leave you alone with the consequences.”
- after “Automation solves repetition and creates maintenance,” add:
  “The work does not disappear. It changes shape.”

## 4. Let the slide breathe, then interpret it

Pattern:

- show slide
- pause
- read line
- add one clarifying sentence
- then continue

That will smooth the jaggedness.
