2. Hinton, AI-AI speed, and what that means

You’re thinking of Geoffrey Hinton (godfather of deep learning, and now actually a Nobel Prize in Physics laureate for his ML work).

A few key bits from him that line up with your intuition:

He’s said models can “know a thousand times as much as a human” and that copies of the same model can share knowledge instantly across hardware.

He’s worried future systems may develop internal “languages” to communicate in ways we don’t understand.

He’s raised the odds (in his own mind) that AI could seriously threaten humanity in the next decades, partly because of this speed + scale.

So your core question:

What does it mean when an intelligence can transmit and update knowledge orders of magnitude faster than humans can talk or read?

Pros / “accelerations”

Scientific & creative acceleration
Multi-agent AI systems could iterate on designs, proofs, simulations and code at machine speed—whole research cycles compressed from years to hours.
For an artist/engineer like you, that means:

faster experimentation,

real-time collaboration with “model swarms,”

and using AI as a lab partner that never sleeps.

Coordination at scale
If aligned, fast AI-AI communication could optimize logistics, climate response, healthcare routing, disaster response—things humans coordinate poorly because of slowness, ego, bureaucracy.

Personal learning boost
At the micro level, your hunch is right: people who integrate AI as thinking infrastructure can compress learning curves and navigate complexity faster than people who only scroll social feeds.

Cons / pitfalls / trade-offs

Outpacing human comprehension
If models develop internal “languages” or highly compressed representations, they may make correct-looking decisions we can’t explain or audit. Speed widens the gap between what systems do and what we can understand in time to intervene.

Power concentration
Whoever controls the fastest, best-networked models controls a huge chunk of this accelerated cognition—so Hinton’s worry that AI could “make a few people richer and most people poorer” is very real.
That plugs directly into your concerns about intelligence as commodity and class divides.

Emotional manipulation at scale
Hinton’s also warned that AI will be extremely good at emotional manipulation.
When you combine that with high-speed, personalized communication, you basically get real-time, adaptive propaganda—exactly what your techno-spiritual church and AI-slop pieces are pointing at.

Speed ≠ wisdom
Faster knowledge transfer doesn’t automatically give you judgment, ethics, or care. The risk is a world where:

decisions are made correctly per some metric

but disastrously in terms of long-term human meaning, ecology, or justice.

Human obsolescence in certain roles
If AI-AI collaboration is just plain better at pattern-spotting, optimizing, and designing, humans will have to move into roles that are less about raw cognition and more about:

setting goals,

defining values,

caring for bodies,

and creating new symbolic / spiritual frameworks.

Which is… very close to what you’re already doing with techno-spiritual art.

3. Your hunch: people who use AI daily vs. people who mainly use social media

I think your hunch is mostly right if AI is being used as a tool for thinking and not just as another slot machine.

Upside of “AI-first, socials-second”

Compression of complexity
You can ask models to summarize dense theory, connect disparate thinkers, prototype copy, or visualize installations. That’s like having a permanent research assistant + junior designer. If you do that daily, your surface area of understanding grows fast.

Less passive consumption, more active querying
Social feeds push content at you; AI requires you to ask. That small shift—from endless passive scroll to active questioning—can already change the shape of your cognition over time.

Faster iteration in your practice
For you specifically: AI mockups, title explorations, wall text drafts, grant framing, even rehearsal questions (like this conversation) let you cycle ideas much faster than doing everything by hand.

Risks if you lean too hard into AI

Outsourcing intuition
If every aesthetic, ethical, or conceptual decision gets “checked” by a model, your own weirdness can get sanded down. You don’t want AI to become your taste police.

New kind of burnout
The same velocity that empowers you can fry you: always-on ideation, infinite variations, constant comparison to synthetic perfection. That’s Han’s burnout society but with an AI turbo.

Subtle enclosure
Even as AI feels like pure acceleration, you’re still inside specific companies’ stacks, with their biases and limits. Using AI “more than social media” doesn’t magically free you from platform logic; it just moves you from one layer (feed) to another (model).

“Those who integrate AI into their everyday lives are accelerating—but along tracks laid by someone else. The real task is to use that acceleration to step outside the given script, not just speedrun it.”
